A permit is not a formality at the end of design. In Greater Vancouver and the Fraser Valley, the application is a project of its own: zoning confirmation, drawings that match what the municipality expects, consultant letters, and a queue that does not move because the owner is ready to build.

## Start with the property, not the mood board

Bring the civic address, a recent survey if you have one, and any existing drawings. Zoning, lot coverage, setbacks, height and parking rules are municipal and site-specific. A multiplex that works in one city can fail two suburbs over. HIC uses that package in the first conversation so feasibility is not guesswork.

## Drawings have to answer the intake checklist

Municipal staff review what you submit, not the conversation you had with a designer. Incomplete civil, structural or energy documentation is a common reason files sit. If you already have a set of plans, include them. If you do not, say so early. Starting from a sketch is normal; pretending a sketch is a permit set is not.

## Time is mostly waiting, then responding

Intake, first comments, resubmission, and a second pass often take longer than construction of a simple renovation. Owners who keep a single point of contact for questions, and who answer staff comments in one coordinated package, move faster than owners who drip-feed revisions.

None of this is a quote for your lot. Confirm requirements with the municipality that will issue the permit. If you want HIC to walk the property and the paperwork with you, [start a conversation](/contact).
