A multiplex is not four copies of a house stacked on a lot. Each extra dwelling changes parking, servicing, fire separations, and how the street reads. Municipal rules in the Fraser Valley are not interchangeable. Confirm what your city currently allows before you buy a rendering.

## Feasibility is a stack of constraints

Lot width, access, existing trees, sewer and hydro capacity, and flood or geotechnical notes can end a scheme that looked fine on paper. Early consultant input costs less than redrawing a full set.

## Who lives there, and who pays for it

Owner-occupy plus rental, a family compound, or a hold-and-rent plan each want different unit mixes and finishes. That choice drives kitchen counts, sound, storage and whether the project still feels like a home. Investment language does not replace liveability. If the building is unpleasant to occupy, it is harder to hold.

## Sequence before marketing

HIC treats multiplex work as a build path beside custom homes: property first, then direction, then drawings, then construction. Use the [estimator](/estimator) only to test whether a floor-area figure sits in a range you can discuss. Then [bring the address](/contact). Zoning still has to be confirmed on that lot.
